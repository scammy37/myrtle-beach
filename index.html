Read before you offer
  • The mortgage is the whole story on cash flow. Self-managed and fully rented, the operating line runs positive — but at ~7.25% financing, debt service still pulls annual cash flow negative. Tap All cash to see it flip green.
  • Appreciation is the real return engine here. At a ~2–3% cap rate, year-one cash yield is thin. The profit at sale is carried by price growth and loan paydown — which is exactly why the appreciation toggle swings the bottom line so hard. NMB is correcting right now, so the flat case isn't paranoid.
  • Property tax nearly doubles at closing — the listing's figure is usually the prior owner's 4% primary rate; you pay the 6% second-home ratio.
  • Self-managing from New Jersey means a local cleaning/turnover crew and someone for guest issues, plus running pricing, listings, and the ~13% accommodations-tax filings yourself.
  • Defaults are grounded estimates, not quotes. Confirm exact HOA dues from the resale certificate and get a real STR insurance quote before trusting the bottom line.
  • This page and the spreadsheet are the same model. The 2BR base case here ties out to the spreadsheet's Base column: $26,000 gross, the same eleven expense lines, the same 25% / 7.25% / 30-yr financing, and cash invested that includes furnishing. The Light and Max presets don't map onto the spreadsheet's Conservative and Optimistic columns — the week presets imply a wider revenue spread than that band.
  • The two files disagree on one number. The spreadsheet carries property tax as a flat $3,000; this page previously derived it from a 0.28 millage, which on a $300k unit gives ~$5,040. Both cite Horry County. The spreadsheet's figure is the default here, and tax is now a slider — run your actual unit on the county estimator and set it yourself.
Not financial advice — a planning tool. Market inputs drawn from AirDNA/AirROI rental data, Coastal Carolinas MLS sold comps, Horry County tax rules, and Aug 2026 mortgage rates. The two numbers that move the result most are your real occupancy and your financing. Accommodations taxes are collected from the guest and remitted — a pass-through, so they're excluded from this owner P&L.
